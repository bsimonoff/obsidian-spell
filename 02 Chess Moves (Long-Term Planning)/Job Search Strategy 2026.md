## Sourcing Strategy: Multi-Channel Attack

### Channel 1: Targeted LinkedIn + Direct Outreach

**Approach:**
1. Research 20–30 companies per industry in target cities
2. Find hiring managers, senior engineers, or technical recruiters on LinkedIn
3. **Send direct message (not just apply online):**
   - "Hi [Name], I'm a recent ChemE grad (Mines, '26) interested in your work in [specific project/area]. I've [capstone/competition project related to their work]. Would you have 10 min to chat about the team/role?"

**Why this works:**
- Shows you did homework (you know their work specifically)
- Direct contact bypasses ATS filters
- Demonstrates genuine interest (not just spray-applying)
- Recent grads who reach out this way get responses

**Target:** Send 2–3 thoughtful messages per day (not 50 generic ones).

---

### Channel 2: Recruiters

**Approach:**
1. Find engineering recruiters specializing in chemical/process engineering
2. Send a concise pitch:
   - "I'm a recent ChemE grad (Mines, 3.64, capstone + design competition background). Looking for: renewable energy/green hydrogen, aerospace, semiconductors. Target salary: $70k+. Flexible on location (Denver, Seattle, NYC, Chicago, Paris, Tokyo, HK). Available to start Oct 2026. Any roles?"
3. Follow up weekly if no response

**Why this works:**
- Recruiters have access to non-advertised roles
- They move fast (commission-driven)
- They handle visa sponsorship logistics

**Target:** Contact 5–10 solid recruiters in your first 2 weeks.

---

### Channel 3: Job Boards + Company Career Pages

**Platforms:**
- **LinkedIn Jobs** (filter by company, "entry-level", location)
- **Green Hydrogen Job Board** (specialized for your primary industry)
- **ACS Career Center** (chemical engineering specific)
- **Indeed, Glassdoor** (broad coverage)
- **Company career pages** directly (Boeing, Lockheed, Siemens, Airbus, Engie, etc.)

**Why:** Capture advertised roles, see what companies are hiring, apply strategically (not randomly).

**Target:** 20–30 applications per week from this channel (be selective, tailor cover letter for each).

---

### Channel 4: Networking

**Approach:**
1. **Mines alumni network:** Find people at target companies, ask for 15-min calls
   - "Hi [Name], I saw you work at [Company] in [city]. I'm graduating in a couple months and interested in [industry]. Would you have 15 minutes to chat about your experience there?"
2. **Professors:** Ask advisors/professors if they have industry connections
   - "I'm targeting renewable energy/aerospace companies. Do you know anyone in industry I could reach out to?"
3. **Competition judges/organizers:** Connect with people from your design competition

**Why:** Warm intros are 10x better than cold applications.

**Target:** 3–5 meaningful conversations per week.

## International Logistics: Key Research

### France
- **Visa:** EU work visa (relatively straightforward for US citizens with job offer)
- **Companies:** Airbus (Toulouse), Engie, Air Liquide, Michelin
- **Advantage:** Bilingual (French) is huge asset
- **Timeline:** Visa process ~2 months
- **Salary:** Similar to USA (~$70k USD equivalent)

### Japan
- **Visa:** Work visa requires employer sponsorship
- **Companies:** Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, Takeda, Daiichi, Rohm, Sony
- **Advantage:** Tier-1 city presence (Tokyo), hydrogen/renewables investment
- **Timeline:** Visa process ~3 months (can take longer)
- **Salary:** Often lower than USA ($50-65k USD equivalent for entry-level)
- **Research:** Not all companies sponsor easily; ask in interviews

### Hong Kong
- **Visa:** Work visa + residence visa (employer-sponsored)
- **Companies:** CITIC, various manufacturing, engineering firms
- **Advantage:** Tier-1 city, Asia hub
- **Timeline:** Visa process ~1-2 months
- **Salary:** Similar to USA (~$70k+ achievable)
- **Research:** Some companies sponsor, some don't; check before applying

**Action:** For each target company, research visa sponsorship track record before applying.

## Interview Preparation Topics

### Technical Prep
- **Process design fundamentals:** How do you approach designing a chemical process?
- **PEMFC basics:** What's a proton exchange membrane fuel cell? Why hydrogen?
- **Ammonia synthesis:** Walk me through your capstone design decisions
- **Cost analysis:** How do you estimate capex and opex?
- **Tools:** Be ready to explain MATLAB, Aspen Plus, Python projects in detail

### Behavioral Prep
- **Why this industry?** (Your story: Why renewable energy? Why aerospace?)
- **Capstone deep dive:** Walk me through your biggest design decision
- **Competition project:** What did you learn? What would you do differently?
- **Environmental work:** What surprised you? How does it shape your approach?
- **Failure:** Tell me about a project that didn't go as planned

### International/Relocation Prep
- **Relocation willingness:** "I'm excited about [City/Country]. I've researched [visa timeline], understand the logistics, and am ready to move."
- **Visa sponsorship:** "I'm open to visa sponsorship. I understand [Japan/France] timelines and am happy to discuss any concerns."
- **Cultural fit:** "I'm interested in how your team approaches [problem relevant to company]."
