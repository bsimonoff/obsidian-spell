## Why This Matters

The **FE (Fundamentals of Engineering) Exam** is:
- ✅ Required first step toward Professional Engineer (PE) licensure
- ✅ A credential that signals competency to employers
- ✅ Available immediately after graduation (you qualify NOW)
- ✅ Increasingly expected in process engineering roles (esp. pharma, aerospace, energy)
- ✅ Valid for life once passed (no expiration)

**Strategic advantage:** Passing the FE before landing a job makes you more competitive and potentially increases starting salary. Some employers see it as a differentiator for early-career hires.

## Exam Overview

### Format
- **Duration:** 5 hours 20 minutes (with breaks)
- **Structure:** 110 multiple-choice questions
  - Morning session: 55 questions (General Engineering)
  - Afternoon session: 55 questions (Chemical Engineering Specialization)
- **Time per question:** ~2.9 minutes average
- **Passing Score:** ~70% (typically 65-75 questions correct)

## Study Tips & Mindset

### Do's
- ✅ **Use NCEES handbook actively.** Master where formulas live; practice finding them under time pressure.
- ✅ **Practice with units.** Unit conversions trip up many engineers. Convert everything to SI or US Customary at the start.
- ✅ **Work problems by hand.** Don't just read solutions; derive them.
- ✅ **Time yourself.** Practice exams must be taken under timed conditions (5h 20m).
- ✅ **Focus on high-weight topics.** Thermo (20%), Fluid Mech (15%), Heat Transfer (12%), Mass Transfer (15%) = 62% of exam.
- ✅ **Form a study group.** Explaining concepts to others cements your understanding.

### Don'ts
- ❌ **Memorize formulas.** You have the NCEES handbook. Master *when* to use them, not *what* they are.
- ❌ **Study every topic equally.** Allocate time proportional to exam weight.
- ❌ **Cram the night before.** Light review only; rest is more valuable.
- ❌ **Overthink during the exam.** If unsure, make an educated guess and move on.
- ❌ **Rush through questions.** You have 2.9 min/question; use it.

## FAQs

**Q: Can I take the exam right after graduation?**  
A: Yes! The FE has no work experience requirement. You're eligible immediately after your ChemE degree.

**Q: How long is the FE valid?**  
A: Forever—once you pass, it's yours. No renewal or continuing education required.

**Q: Can I retake the exam if I fail?**  
A: Yes, you can retake it. Most states allow multiple attempts (check Colorado board). Retake costs another exam fee (~$175).

**Q: Do employers care if I have the FE?**  
A: Yes, especially for process engineering roles in regulated industries (pharma, aerospace, energy). Some roles prefer or require it. It signals competency and career intentionality.

**Q: Is the FE hard?**  
A: It's moderate difficulty. Not a barrier for someone who understood their ChemE coursework. Most graduates pass on first attempt if they study for 4-6 weeks.

**Q: What calculator do I need?**  
A: **Only approved calculators are allowed:**
  - TI-36X Pro (most common)
  - Casio FX-991EX or FX-991DE X
  - Do NOT use graphing calculators (TI-84, etc.)
  - Buy or borrow the approved calculator NOW; practice with it
