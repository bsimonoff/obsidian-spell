> **Purpose:** Make measurable learning speed your headline differentiator across resume, cover letters, and interviews. See [[CLAUDE.md]] → "My Strengths & Weaknesses" for context.
> **Why this is your edge:** New grads compete on *potential and ramp speed*, not years of experience. Yours is demonstrably above average — so lead with it instead of hiding it.

---

## The core message (one sentence, memorize it)

> **"I ramp fast — once I understand *why* a process works, I can run with it, adapt it, and improve it without hand-holding."**

That's your thesis. Everything below is proof of it.

### The evidence behind the claim (so it's not a hollow line)
- Self-taught the FE exam to a consistent **85–90%** (vs. ~50–56% pass line), correcting weak spots after a **single** exposure and transferring the fix to new problem formats.
- Capstone: modeled reactor kinetics and mass transport from fundamentals to optimize a full ammonia synthesis plant.
- PEMFC competition project: built a fuel-cell system design by integrating electrochemistry with real engineering constraints — a domain not spoon-fed in coursework.
- Internship: caught missing/inaccurate samples others missed — fast pattern recognition applied to a live process.

## Interview framing

### "Tell me about yourself" (your 30-second open)
> "I'm a chemical engineer out of Colorado School of Mines. What I've learned about myself is that I ramp fast — I don't just follow procedures, I dig into *why* they work, which lets me pick up new tools and processes quickly and start improving them. I saw it in my capstone teaching myself reactor modeling, and again self-studying the FE. I'm looking for a role where that fast ramp is an asset — somewhere I can get productive quickly and grow."

### "What's your greatest strength?"
> "Ramp speed. Once I understand the mechanism behind a process, I move fast and independently. Concrete example: [FE self-study / capstone kinetics / picking up Aspen]. I close the gap between 'new to this' and 'contributing' faster than most."

### "What's your greatest weakness?" (be honest — it's more credible, and you know yours)
> "Communication has been a growth area — I used to assume people were tracking my thinking. I've been deliberate about over-communicating: writing things down, checking alignment early. I'd rather flag it than have it surprise a team."
*(This is real, from your own notes — owning it beats a fake weakness, and it shows self-awareness.)*

## The one discipline note
Your documented risk isn't ability — it's **procrastination/overthinking under stress** and **under-communicating**. In interviews that shows up as *underselling*. Don't. You have measured evidence you ramp fast; state it plainly and back it with a story. Confidence here is accuracy, not bragging.
